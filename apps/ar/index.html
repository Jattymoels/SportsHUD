A head test: recording the sensors

This is how the heading work gets tested against a real head. It needs no route and shows nothing: it only writes down what the motion sensors and GPS report while you run.

How to do a head test
  1. Fix the phone firmly to your head, screen outwards: a head strap with a phone clamp, or tight under a headband. It must not wobble. Which way round does not matter.
  2. Before putting it on, tap "Start a head test" and allow location and motion. The screen goes dark and ignores touches.
  3. Stand still for ten seconds, facing the way you will set off.
  4. Run for about ten minutes, anywhere: a few corners, and one straight of 200 m or more. Look where you are going, as you normally would.
  5. Then, on a straight, run for a minute looking well to one side, as if at someone beside you.
  6. Stop and stand still for ten seconds. Take it off, then press and hold the screen with two fingers for two seconds.
  7. Tap "Download the recording" and send the file. It holds where you ran, and stays on the phone until you send it. If the browser drops the page part-way, what was written is kept, and the test carries on by itself when the page comes back.
What this is, and how to use it
  • This page draws only what glasses would add to your view: the ghost standing on the road where it is, a line along the route, a gate at each marker. Everything else is black, which glasses show as nothing.
  • Which way your head faces comes from the device's motion sensors, with no compass. It is kept true by the way you run: over a minute, the middle of where your head points is the way the route goes.
  • On glasses that run web pages: open this page on them. Set how much of your view the display covers.
  • With a phone in a see-through visor: set the pictures to suit the visor (one or two, flipped if it shows the screen in a mirror), then "Wear it".
  • Lining up: stand on the route, look level along it, and tap the picture or press Enter. That tells it how the device sits on your head and which way the route runs. Tap again whenever the line has wandered off the road.
  • A pace ghost needs no route. It holds its pace wherever you run, and is drawn ahead of you the way you are going. There is nothing to line up to at the start, so tap to start, set off looking where you are going, and it lines up by itself after a few seconds. Where your way joins the route of a saved run, that run's ghost takes over on its own route until you leave it.
  • Close up and stopped. Nearer than about 7 m the ghost is too tall to fit, and becomes a head and shoulders at the bottom of the picture. When you stop, everything placed in the world fades right down, and comes back as you set off.
  • Keys, for glasses or a small remote: Enter lines up. Left and right turn the picture a degree. Up and down raise and lower it.
  • Routes come from runs saved by Ghost run on this device, a GPX added here, or a link someone sent.
  • Try it without going anywhere, on a sample run: a pace ghost with no route, a pace ghost that hands over to a saved run where the two routes meet, or a saved run on its own route. Drag to turn your head.
