Golf HUD

Silent, glanceable course guidance: how far to the green, where the chosen club lands the ball in today's wind, and the distance to whatever you look at. On the 18 holes of the Grange that OpenStreetMap has mapped, or a made-up hole. At the desk the sliders say where you are; on the course the phone can.

On the glasses

Keys, as on the glasses: Enter moves to the next screen. On the Club screen, Left and Right change club and Up and Down move the aim.

Where you are, and the day

Asks Open-Meteo, a public forecast service, which is sent roughly where the course is.

The numbers behind the screen

Course test check the numbers against a rangefinder; kept on this device

A laser gives the distance to the flag, wherever the hole is cut today. So either stand at the flag and press "I am at today's pin" first, then check "pin" from anywhere on that hole; or check "centre" against the course's distance markers.

Everything logged

The bag carry in metres, in still air; changes are kept on this device

What is real here

  • The distances are worked out the way they will be on a course: from a position and a mapped outline of the green and hazards. Front and back of the green are along the line from where you stand through its middle.
  • The Grange layout comes from OpenStreetMap, which has a line of play, par and stroke index for 18 of the club's 36 holes, with their greens, pins, tees, fairways and bunkers. They are on the east side of the property. Nothing has yet been checked against the ground: the pin is wherever the mapper put it, not today's hole.
  • The made-up hole is laid out on a grid, so each number can be checked by hand. "Put the made-up hole where I am standing" lays it on the ground from your position, playing the way you face, so the page can be tried anywhere: walk towards the green and watch the numbers fall.
  • The wind's effect is a rule of thumb: a headwind costs about 1% of carry per mph and a tailwind gives about half that, and a crosswind moves the ball sideways in proportion to carry. It moves the zone the right way; the amounts are placeholders until there are real shots to learn from.
  • The club distances are the ones entered for this bag, the middle of each range. Inside the shortest club's distance the zone is drawn for a part shot of the length needed.
  • Your position, when the phone supplies it, stays in the browser.
  • Looking down past 40° for a third of a second hides everything, as at address. Lift your head and it returns.
